## Sizing

A worker needs up to `concurrency` connections for settles that happen at once, plus one for its
claim loop and one for heartbeats, plus whatever its handlers query. Size the pool at
`poolOptions.max ≥ min(concurrency, 10) + 2`.

The trap is a heartbeat queued behind a saturated pool: a heartbeat later than `leaseMs` lets the
lease expire and the message runs twice. `controlDb`, a second small `pgPrime` instance passed in
`WorkerOptions`, takes the claims, heartbeats and expiry scans off the main pool.

## Wake-ups and the NOTIFY ceiling

Sends, promotions, retries, released parents and freed group or global capacity send a NOTIFY on
an engine channel; idle workers wake on it, on a LISTEN gap, or after `pollMs`. PostgreSQL
serializes the commits of notifying transactions, so a very hot producer can set
`pgMq({ notify: false })` and let workers poll. Measured on a laptop PostgreSQL 17 (`bench/mq`),
processing rates were within noise of each other with NOTIFY on and off.

## Behind PgBouncer

| | Direct or session pooler | PgBouncer transaction mode |
|---|---|---|
| sends, claims, settles, flows, bindings, `migrate` | ✓ | ✓ |
| wake-ups, `watch()`, `result()` | ✓ | only with pg-prime's `directConnection`; otherwise workers poll every `pollMs`, `result()` re-reads every `pollMs`, and `watch()` is refused |

## Retention and vacuum

Finished messages carry an `expire_at` from the queue's `keep` setting (completed: 1 day, failed
and cancelled: 7 days). Each worker's maintenance, gated to one per queue per `maintenanceMs`,
deletes expired rows and rows beyond `keep.{completed,failed}.count` in batches of at most 1 000,
reconciles the group and queue counters, and drops expired dedup windows.

`keep.completed: false` deletes a message at its completion; its result still reaches a flow
parent. `messages` is created with `fillfactor = 70` and aggressive autovacuum thresholds:
heartbeats, progress and counter updates touch no indexed column, so they stay HOT updates.

## Admin

`pause()` stops every new claim; `drop()` refuses while a message is active unless `force`, and
stops this instance's workers and bindings of the queue before deleting it, without waiting for
in-flight handlers (`force` aborts their signals), so a handler may drop its own queue.
`list({ state: 'failed' })`, `retry({ state: 'failed' })` and `purge({ states: ['failed'] })`
manage the dead-letter state. Every statement is labelled `mq.<op>` in `db.observe`, the slow log
and OpenTelemetry spans; `spanAttributes(e)` maps a settle to the messaging attributes.
